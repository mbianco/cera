# ADR-002: Experiment as a first-class entity

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-14
**Spec references:** `resolutions.md` R2; `domain-model.md` C7;
`ubiquitous-language.md` Experiment [CANDIDATE -> CORE];
`assumptions.md` U6 (resolved: yes)

## Context

The original domain model marked "Experiment" as `[CANDIDATE]` — a
scientific investigation consisting of one or more Workflows and
CESM Cases, tied together by a research question. The analyst asked
the domain expert whether scientists think in terms of Experiments
that span multiple Workflows and CESM Cases (`assumptions.md` U6).

Without confirmation, the architect cannot design Experiment as a
first-class entity: persistence, querying, and ownership constraints
all depend on whether scientists actually use this concept. If the
answer is "no," Experiment is just informal vocabulary and does not
need a type, a service, or a persistence model.

The domain expert resolved this (R2): **yes, Experiment is a
first-class entity above Workflow.**

## Decision

Experiment is a `[CORE]` entity in C7 (Agent Interaction). It is the
top-level organizational unit for a scientist's work.

### Fields

- `name` — human-readable name (e.g., "CO2 doubling sensitivity
  study")
- `researchQuestion` — the scientific question tying the Experiment's
  Workflows and Cases together
- `workflowIds: WorkflowId[]` — referenced Workflows (by identity)
- `caseIds: CaseId[]` — referenced CESM Cases (by identity)
- `createdAt: Date`

### Ownership constraints

- A Workflow belongs to exactly one Experiment.
- A Case belongs to exactly one Experiment.
- **Enforcement model: eventual consistency (FINDING-02
  resolution).** `tool-invocation.createCase()` accepts
  `experimentId` **without synchronous validation** — the
  dependency graph (Phase 4 → Phase 5) prohibits `tool-invocation`
  from importing `agent-interaction`. Validation happens
  asynchronously when `agent-interaction.addCaseToExperiment()` is
  called (by the Workflow or by the User). If the Experiment does
  not exist, `ExperimentNotFound` is thrown and the User is
  notified. The Case is not destroyed — it remains in C1 with its
  `experimentId` until the Experiment is created or the User
  reassigns it.
- `agent-interaction.createWorkflow()` validates `experimentId`
  synchronously (it has access to `ExperimentService`).

### Lifecycle

create -> add Workflows -> add Cases -> query. An Experiment is
long-lived: it survives Session end, like Workflow and
ProvenanceRecord.

### Not an aggregate root

Experiment is a grouping entity that references Workflows and Cases
by identity. It is NOT a DDD aggregate root because:
- Workflows and Cases have their own consistency boundaries and
  independent lifecycles.
- Making them children of Experiment would create an overly large
  aggregate and complicate cross-Session access.
- The Experiment does not enforce consistency on its children; it
  only groups them.

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Scientists can organize work by research question, not just by
  individual Workflows.
- Cross-Workflow queries are possible ("show me all Workflows in the
  CO2 doubling sensitivity study").
- The Experiment entity provides narrative context that a Workflow
  alone cannot.

**Negative:**
- One more entity to model, persist, and query.
- The ownership constraint (Workflow belongs to exactly one
  Experiment) adds a validation step to `createWorkflow()` and
  `createCase()`.

## Alternatives

### Alternative A: No Experiment entity
Workflows and Cases are the top-level organizational units;
"experiment" is informal vocabulary. Rejected by the domain expert
(R2): scientists do think in terms of Experiments.

### Alternative B: Experiment as an aggregate root
Make Workflows and Cases children of the Experiment aggregate. This
would mean the Experiment must load all its Workflows and Cases to
enforce consistency, which is impractical for long-running CESM
experiments with hundreds of output Datasets. Rejected: Workflows
and Cases have independent lifecetimes and consistency boundaries.
